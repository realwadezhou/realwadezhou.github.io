## Collection method

Searches covered product families and years, including successful launches, misses, price/name discrepancies, cancellations, and revised timelines. Original 9to5Mac Gurman articles and a direct interview were used where accessible. Most Bloomberg claims were recovered through contemporaneous MacRumors or other public reports with explicit attribution. The date shown is the consulted report date, not necessarily the earliest forecast date. The source note records known display-date/URL conflicts and coauthors. The October 2023 USB-C accessory report card is a post-event lead and remains Not assessed.

Each row is a short normalized paraphrase. A single report can yield several feature conditions and a timing condition. Those rows are correlated, and some bundles overlap. Revisions remain in the same family. The original claim is not silently replaced by a later correction.

A supported product detail receives no invented calendar credit. A missed deadline remains contradicted after a later launch. Reports of prototype testing are not disproved merely because the feature failed to ship. Ambiguous possibility words, retail names, approximate measurements, and season boundaries retain their limitations. The M4 Air spring-2025 forecast is Unresolvable because reasonable seasonal readings change the verdict. The sub-$500 iPhone forecast remains Not assessed because its attribution needs recovery.

## Findings and practical interpretation

The record contains many supported hardware details: the single USB-C MacBook, Touch Bar and Touch ID, Face ID iPad redesign, M4 Mac mini dimensions/chips, and AirPods product segmentation. Clear misses include Reality Pro branding, the flat-edged Series 7 design, wireless charging for the 2022 iPad Pro, and some chip/camera specifications. Timing misses span the first Watch, second-generation Watch, HomePod, 2020 AirTags, 2022 Mac Pro, and mid-2025 AirTag 2.

The M3 MacBook Pro sequence illustrates why revisions matter: the October 16, 2023 report pointed into early/spring 2024; the October 22 report moved to October 30–31. Apple announced the product October 30. Retaining both prevents the correction from erasing the earlier miss. These examples motivate separate feature and timing evaluation; they do not establish the success probability of his next report.

## Apple launch reference

The catalog retains distinct announcement, order, and customer/store milestones for major product generations plus selected services. Dates are US/initial-market dates. First announcements can precede the launch year, as with the original Watch and Vision Pro. Each populated date binds to a specific source URL. Null is unknown, not announcement day. A week-level date is shown as a week, and a future availability date remains scheduled. General launch dates exclude isolated early deliveries where noted.

Apple newsroom references are the primary evidence; a small number of contemporaneous reports supplement exact availability and executive statements. Source titles, publication dates, access dates, and response hashes are stored. A hash of a currently retrieved page is not a historical archive. The table is a reference snapshot, not a live monitor.

## Limits and remaining coverage gaps

Coverage is uneven and search-selected. This is not every newsletter, tweet, major SKU, or product launch. iPhone and Mac supply most claims. iPod forecasts, full original Bloomberg text, a systematic canceled-project timeline, more recent 2025–2026 reporting, and independent review are not complete. The absence of a launch in this table is never sufficient proof of non-occurrence; disputed/negative cases need independent supporting evidence.

No original paywalled article is represented as read merely because a secondary source links to it. Internal project cancellation reporting is labeled as reported context when it comes from the same forecaster. No human second-review status is claimed.

## Maintenance

Read the existing launch catalog before searching for dates. Add a source-backed launch once, then reference its stable ID in each forecast. Append new forecasts rather than rewriting an older prediction with its correction. Record substantive assessment changes in history. Re-run the generator(s), evidence/date tests, type/lint checks, and production build after changes. Revisit the cutoff date and future/scheduled statuses deliberately.
